    2A. Numerical features¶

    Figure 1 - conclusion. - age is unimodal and mildly right-skewed (skew 0.69). The mean (40.89) is above the median (39.00), and the mode is 32. 95.50% of clients are 25-60. A thin tail above about 60 holds the IQR outliers (394, 1.09%), which are real older clients. - balance is extremely right-skewed (skew 8.34). The mean of 1,365.49 is three times the median of 451.00, and there is a spike at exactly 0 (7.77% of clients). 8.30% are negative (min -8,019) and the max is 102,127. The plot is clipped to P1-P99 [-626, 13,161] for readability. - day has no skew (0.09) and no outliers, but it is multimodal, with peaks on specific days (20 (6.1%), 18 (5.0%), 21 (4.5%)) and uneven counts across the month. This reflects call scheduling rather than a smooth quantity.

    Figure 2 - conclusion. - campaign is a heavy-tailed count (skew 4.94). The mode is 1 contact (38.78% of clients), 78.69% received at most 3, and 945 clients received more than 10 (max 63). - pdays applies only to previously contacted clients (18.20%). Its distribution is multimodal (median 195.00, IQR 133.00-328.00). The most frequent values are 182 (128), 92 (114), 91 (100), 183 (95), 181 (92) days, i.e. clients re-contacted about three or six months after the previous campaign. Figure 2 also shows a further cluster just under 400 days. - previous among contacted clients has a median of 2.00 and a mode of 1, with a very long tail up to 275 (skew 28.55).

    Figure 3 - conclusion. Using the 1.5 x IQR rule: - balance has the most real outliers (3,770, 10.42%), on both sides. campaign has 2,465 (6.82%) and age 394 (1.09%). - For pdays and previous, the IQR collapses to the single sentinel value (Q1 = Q3 = -1.00 and 0.00). Every previously contacted client (6,584) is therefore flagged as an "outlier". This is an artifact of the sentinel, not evidence of bad data. Within contacted clients, there are only 40 outliers for pdays and 367 for previous. - 4 features have |skew| > 1 (balance, campaign, pdays, previous). This motivates rank-based correlation in 3A, and transforms (log/Yeo-Johnson, or binning) and/or robust scaling in Part 2.

    Figure 4 - conclusion. - job is dominated by blue-collar (21.6%), management (20.8%) and technician (16.8%) clients. - marital is mostly married (60.2%), and education mostly secondary (51.3%). - default is almost constant (98.2% no). - month is concentrated in May-August (71.4% of contacts; May alone is 30.6%). March, September, October and December are thin (1.1%, 1.2%, 1.6%, 0.5%). - poutcome is 81.8% "unknown", which is the "never contacted" group.

    Figure 5 - conclusion. - No feature has high cardinality. The maximum is 12 levels (job, month), so one-hot encoding is feasible. - Rare levels (< 1% of training rows): job: unknown; month: dec. March, September and October are also small (just over 1%). Their subscription-rate estimates are noisier (see the wide CIs in Figure 9), so Part 2 may consider grouping them if a model overfits. - "unknown" is substantial in poutcome and contact, small in education, and marginal in job. It is kept as a category (1B).
